Water Conservation Heritage

Traditional Water Management

For generations, rural communities across the Bhandara–Gondia–Vidarbha region developed practical systems for storing, distributing and using water. Tanks, embankments, channels and spillways supported agriculture and village life, especially in a landscape dependent on seasonal rainfall.

Historical Interpretation Note

Water systems described in historical sources were created and maintained within the administrative, agricultural and environmental conditions of their time. Individual tanks may have different histories and builders. Community association should therefore be stated only where supported by documentary or clearly identified community evidence.

Malguzari Tanks

Agrarian Water Systems

Large numbers of traditional irrigation tanks existed across the historic Central Provinces, particularly in the Bhandara and adjoining regions. These water bodies supported agriculture, livestock and village settlements.

Historical Terminology: Historical administrative records and community literature use the term 'Malguzari tanks' in connection with village-level land and water systems.

Kohli Water-Management Tradition

Community Heritage

Historical descriptions of the Kohli community in the Central Provinces associate sections of the community with agriculture, irrigation and the construction or maintenance of water works. The available sources describe practical knowledge of tanks and irrigation systems, developed through experience and adapted to local conditions.

Historical Description

One historical settlement description notes that the Kohlis were particularly associated with the cultivation of sugarcane and were found in areas where the ground conditions were favourable for large tanks. It also describes their practical knowledge and disciplined community organization.

Source: Historical Settlement Material

Traditional Tank Engineering

Historical water storage across the region relied on time-tested principles of topography, soil compaction, stone reinforcement and gravity-flow irrigation.

Earthen Embankments

Compacted earth was used to create strong embankments that retained seasonal water.

Stone Structures

Stone was used in selected sections where greater strength and resistance to water flow was required.

Spillways

Spillways allowed excess water to leave the tank safely during heavy rainfall.

Water Channels

Channels distributed stored water towards agricultural fields.

Sluice / Outlet Systems

Controlled outlets helped regulate the release of stored water.

Irrigation Distribution

Stored water could be distributed to agricultural land according to local arrangements.

Water and Agriculture

Reliable water storage enabled cultivation of water-dependent crops such as rice and sugarcane. Historical material also connects Kohli agricultural practice with sugarcane cultivation and jaggery production.

Regional Water Heritage

Navegaon Bandh

Prominent Water Landmark

Navegaon Bandh is part of the wider water and agricultural landscape of the Bhandara–Gondia region. Its presence illustrates the long-standing importance of stored water to agriculture and rural settlements.

Community traditions associate local water heritage with historic tanks across the district, while documentary sources describe the collective agrarian importance of such reservoirs.

Water as a Community Responsibility

Traditional village water systems depended not only on construction but also on collective maintenance, regulation and seasonal use. Community cooperation was essential for keeping irrigation systems functional.

Water and Village Life

Village tanks supported multiple interconnected dimensions of rural society, economy and ecology:

Agriculture

Essential irrigation for staple paddy crops and sugarcane plots during dry spells.

Livestock

Year-round drinking and bathing water for cattle, buffaloes and agricultural livestock.

Household Needs

Non-potable domestic water requirements, washing, and traditional village routines.

Village Ecology

Sustaining local groundwater levels, regional wells, aquatic flora, and bird habitats.

Knowledge Passed Through Generations

Much of the traditional knowledge surrounding tanks, water flow, soil, crops and seasonal management was practical knowledge transmitted through experience and generations.

Historical sources and oral traditions should be read together, while keeping documented evidence and community memory clearly distinguished.

From Heritage to Conservation

Traditional water systems remain relevant to contemporary discussions about water conservation, groundwater recharge, agricultural resilience and restoration of village water bodies.
