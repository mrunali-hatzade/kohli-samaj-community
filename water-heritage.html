1. Traditional Reservoirs

Historical sources document the construction of large water bodies designed to trap seasonal rainfall and prevent dry-season droughts.

2. Malguzari Water Systems

Traditional revenue-management and water systems, known as Malguzari, supported community-based agricultural prosperity across Chandrapur, Gadchiroli, Bhandara, and Nagpur.

3. Agriculture & Irrigation

The development of extensive irrigation channels helped support demanding crops such as rice and sugarcane in the regional valleys.

4. Community Water Management

Local networks relied heavily on community participation, village labor, and collective maintenance of bunds and sluice gates.

5. Traditional Engineering Knowledge

Surveys of older reservoirs reveal highly sophisticated earthworks, spillways, and stone pitchings, engineered successfully without modern surveying instruments.

Traditional water systems were closely connected with agriculture, village life and the management of seasonal water resources.

Water Management Supporting Agriculture

Early historical documentations from the Central Provinces highlight the Kohli community's traditional role in cultivation. The massive water-management networks they constructed, including thousands of tanks and bhandaras, were designed specifically to support agriculture, enabling the successful cultivation of demanding crops such as sugarcane and rice. This integration of water engineering and agriculture remains a cornerstone of the community's heritage.

Kohli Agricultural Water Management (Chanda Settlement)

Historical settlement records describe the construction of large reservoirs to support irrigation and sugarcane cultivation during the long hot months.
