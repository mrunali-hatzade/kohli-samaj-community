Culture, Traditions & Festivals

Pola, Tansen, traditional agrarian festivals, kuldaivats, and vibrant social customs

Vibrant Traditions & Community Values

The Kohli Samaj celebrates rich cultural festivals reflecting deep agricultural roots and respect for nature. From Pola (worship of farm cattle) to Diwali and traditional folk music dances, our traditions foster strong unity.

Key Traditions:
  • Bail Pola Festival & Agricultural Gratitude
  • Vadhu-Var Parichay Melava (Matrimonial Conventions)
  • Folk Songs & Village Cultural Gatherings

Traditional Marriage Practices

Cultural Heritage

Role of Family & Elders

Traditionally, marriage discussions involved parents, relatives and respected community elders to ensure social harmony.

Agricultural Calendar Influence

Marriage planning was traditionally influenced by agricultural harvesting seasons and family availability after paddy work.

Tulsi Vivah & Chaturmas

Highlighting the traditional significance of the period between Ashadhi Ekadashi and Tulsi Vivah in cultural practice.

Community Participation & Hospitality

Traditional weddings involved relatives, guests, village elders, and maintained respectful social hospitality.

Note: These practices are historical references illustrating cultural traditions and are not presented as mandatory requirements today.

Changing Social Practices

Contemporary Evolution

Over recent decades, increased access to higher education, professional career opportunities, and urbanization have naturally evolved social traditions within the Kohli community. Women's higher education and career pursuits, movement of youth to major metropolitan centers for employment, and contemporary digital matchmaking forums have expanded family networks and broadened opportunities.

"The Kohli community continues to balance the preservation of traditional cultural values with the changing realities of education, careers, urbanisation and contemporary family life."
