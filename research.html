The Historiographical & Research Library of the Kohli Samaj provides an organized, evidence-based repository of historical documentation relating to the community in the Central Provinces and Berar. Drawing upon nineteenth-century official gazetteers, cadastral settlement records, decennial census volumes, colonial ethnographic treatises, and twentieth-century community monographs, the library establishes an objective empirical foundation for studying eastern Vidarbha’s agrarian and social development.

Historical scholarship on the Kohli community is marked by a distinctive convergence between colonial administrative reports—which documented their unparalleled mastery over tank construction, sugarcane cultivation, and village Malguzari titles—and indigenous community memoirs which preserved oral genealogies, clan deities, social conventions, and marital traditions. This portal applies rigorous critical standards, separating primary administrative records from oral memory while documenting both with bibliographic integrity.

Russell & Hira Lal Ethnographic Survey (1916)

Published in 1916 by Macmillan & Co., London, The Tribes and Castes of the Central Provinces of India by R. V. Russell and Rai Bahadur Hira Lal constitutes the benchmark colonial ethnographic survey of the province. In Volume III, the authors dedicated an extensive monograph to the Kohli community:

  • Agrarian Pre-eminence: Russell described the Kohlis as master cultivators and irrigation engineers who constructed the celebrated Navegaon Bandh and hundreds of earthen tanks throughout the Wainganga valley.
  • Sugarcane Cultivation: Documented their introduction and sustained cultivation of the superior Kathani sugarcane variety, operated through communal crushing apparatus and boiling installations.
  • Village Headships (Patils): Recorded that substantial Kohli families held hereditary proprietary rights (Malguzari) and village headships (Patilki) in numerous villages across Bhandara and Chanda districts.
Critical Historiographical Context:

Twentieth-century colonial ethnographic surveys employed hierarchical classificatory frameworks characteristic of imperial administrative documentation. Scholars analyze these texts for their empirical observations on agrarian and hydraulic practices while recognizing the historical boundaries of colonial sociological models.

Demographic Records: The 11,399 & 19,739 Benchmarks

Two historical demographic benchmarks recur consistently across colonial archives and scholarly publications, representing distinct administrative scopes:

1911 Bhandara Census Benchmark 11,399

Official decennial census enumeration recorded strictly within the historical boundaries of Bhandara District (which then included present-day Gondia district).

Combined Settlement Record 19,739

Combined revenue settlement population across Bhandara (12,097) and Chanda (7,602) districts, encompassing Sakoli (8,130) and Brahmapuri (6,578) tehsils.

Community Publications, Smrutigranths & Monographic Studies

During the post-independence era, community intellectuals and social forums initiated systematic documentation of oral history, village genealogies, and cultural traditions. The most comprehensive academic contribution is Ramdas Borkar’s monographic treatise Kohli Samaj Itihas va Sanskruti (2001), which surveyed 518 villages across eastern Vidarbha.

Cultural Traditions, Rituals & Ethnology

Ethnological research on the community documents distinctive social practices including Tulsi Vivah community worship, ancestral kuldaivat rituals (such as Khandoba and Bhavani), and communal gatherings at Dahaljit platforms, reflecting strong social coherence.

Hydraulic Engineering & Malguzari Tank Studies

Modern civil and environmental engineering studies have highlighted the sophisticated ecological balance achieved by traditional Kohli tank systems. Through cascading overflow catchments, earthen embankments with rip-rap stone pitching, and gravity-fed stone sluice conduits (Khind), these historical works preserved groundwater without electric power.

Agrarian Cultivars, Sugarcane & Paddy Heritage

Agrarian records demonstrate that Kohli cultivators systematically introduced and propagated superior fragrant rice strains including Chinnor, Luchai, and Dubraj, alongside high-sucrose Kathani sugarcane. Their transplantation method (Aavni) became the standard agricultural technique adopted across the Central Provinces.

Historical Methodology: The Four Interpretive Principles

To ensure scholarly rigor, this portal adheres to four core historiographical principles when consulting archival sources:

1. Institutional Context

Analyze the fiscal, legal, or administrative mandate governing why a colonial document was commissioned.

2. Historical Semantics

Distinguish nineteenth-century fiscal classifications from modern constitutional and statutory definitions.

3. Geographic Demarcation

Account for shifting district, tehsil, and pargana boundaries across decennial census years.

4. Source Triangulation

Cross-reference oral community memoirs with independent archival surveys to corroborate historical claims.
