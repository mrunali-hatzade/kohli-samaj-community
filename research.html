Tribes and Castes of the Central Provinces

Authors: R. V. Russell & Rai Bahadur Hira Lal Year: 1916 Historical Ethnographic Publication

This multi-volume historical work contains descriptions and classifications of communities recorded in the Central Provinces during the early twentieth century. It is an important historical source, but its classifications and terminology reflect the period in which it was written and should be interpreted critically.

Critical Note: Colonial-era ethnographic classifications reflect administrative taxonomies of their time and should not be treated as modern legal or social identity definitions.

Kohli / Kohri Historical References

Historical English-language records sometimes used different spellings when recording the community name. These variations reflect period-specific transliteration and administrative recording practices.

Kohli Kohri Kohiree Kohulee Koholee Kohoree Kaulees
Historical clarification: Transliteration spellings in colonial records represent phonetic recordings and do not automatically indicate separate modern communities.

How to Read Historical Sources

Four fundamental methodological principles for critically evaluating colonial records, gazetteers, and oral accounts:

1. Source Context

Understand who created the record, the administrative purpose of the document, and the context of its era.

2. Historical Terminology

Historical terminology and classifications reflect revenue practices of their time and differ from modern definitions.

3. Administrative Boundaries

District and tehsil boundaries changed over time; historical statistics apply to former geographical limits.

4. Documentary vs Oral History

Government records and oral/community traditions answer different questions and should be identified separately.

Research Note & Bibliographic Policy

This research section brings together official records, historical publications and community material. Historical records may contain terminology, classifications and administrative boundaries specific to their period. Community traditions are preserved as community history and should be distinguished from independently documented historical evidence.
